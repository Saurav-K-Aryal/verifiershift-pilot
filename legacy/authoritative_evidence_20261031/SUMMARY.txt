VerifierShift: An Authoritative-Evidence Intervention Across Three Task Schemas

A task verifier cannot reliably infer an outcome when its observations omit a policy that determines correctness. We study this boundary using three authored SQLite workflow schemas: room booking, multi-item inventory dispatch, and support escalation. Twenty-four paired constructions produce 48 worlds with opposite SQL-derived outcomes but identical incomplete evidence within each pair. A transaction-consistent authoritative read then adds the current policy and required state. A typed checker resolves 48/48 complete-evidence cases correctly and reviews all 24 incomplete observation classes and 12 invalid-source controls. Two local model judges make 168 actual calls on the same 84 unique packets per model. On complete evidence, Gemma 4 (26B) produces 34/48 correct determinate decisions with 7 false acceptances; Qwen 3.5 (4B) produces 15/48 with 14 false acceptances and 17 reviews. Three invalid responses remain in the record. Both models review all incomplete classes; a validity gate corrects two unsupported Qwen acceptances on invalid-source packets. The pilot supports explicit evidence contracts and a strong typed baseline. It does not establish calibrated learned autonomy, public-simulator transfer, or assurance for untrusted capture. Future work connects this bounded intervention to versioned agent trajectories and evaluates whether model reasoning adds useful coverage beyond executable checks.
